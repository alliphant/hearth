    How the board works
    
    
    You're the tech lead; Beatrice is the scrum master. Click any card to move it, score it, archive it, or start developing it — or just tell Beatrice in the chat rail (she can do all of it too). Every change is audited either way. “Start developing” hands a backend epic to Beatrice's build pipeline: she opens a reviewed PR — nothing merges without your approval.
    

    
    The five lanes
    
    
    Backlog → Sprint → In Progress → Review → Done. Every move is logged — that's what fills the activity heatmap.
    

    
    How ranking works
    
    
      
      
    • Features rank by ROI = value ÷ effort (S/M/L = 1/3/5). Quadrants: Quick Win, Strategic, Fill-in, Drop.
      • 
      
    • Bugs rank by severity — a critical bug always tops the list; high/medium/low interleave with features by ROI.
      • 
      
    • Unscored items are flagged (⚑), never silently ranked — score them before grooming.
      • 
    
    

    
    The ceremonies — just ask Beatrice
    
    
      
      
    • Standup — say standup → say/do, what's in progress, the ranked next-up.
      • 
      
    • Groom — say groom the board → she ranks the backlog and recommends a sprint, then stops and files the commit as a proposal for you to approve.
      • 
      
    • Capture — add a feature: dark mode (M, high value) or add a bug: login crashes on launch (critical, ios).
      • 
      
    • Decisions — when she's blocked on a judgment call she files it as a proposal; it appears under “Awaiting you” and pushes to your phone.
      • 
      
    • Retro — say retro at sprint close to capture what went well / slipped / lessons.
      • 
    
    

    
    Say / Do
    
    
    Committed points vs shipped points — the most honest number on the board. Treat capacity seriously: better to ship 2 epics fully than commit 5 half-done.
    

    
    Backend vs iOS
    
    
    One sprint, one capacity pool, split across the two Hearth repos. The split bar + heatmap show where the work is going.
