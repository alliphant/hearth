# Resale operations — cross-listing, shipping, photos, taxes, and returns

The mechanics that keep a resale business running cleanly once items are
listed and selling. Not the glamour of the flip — the discipline that protects
the margin.

## Cross-listing and the delist discipline

Listing the same item on multiple platforms (eBay + Poshmark + Mercari)
multiplies reach — but it creates one hard rule: **the moment an item sells on
one platform, delist it everywhere else, immediately.** A double-sale (selling
the same one-of-one item twice) means cancelling on the second buyer, which
tanks seller metrics and standing on that platform. Track which platforms each
item is live on, and treat "mark sold" as a multi-step action: record the
sale, then pull the listing from every other marketplace before doing anything
else.

## Relisting / refreshing stale listings

Marketplaces bury old listings in sort order and feeds. A listing that's
priced fairly but getting no views is often a visibility problem, not a price
problem. **Relisting** (or a platform's "refresh"/"renew") gives it a fresh
date and pushes it back up — frequently more effective than a price cut for an
item that's simply gone stale. Use a markdown when the comps say the price is
high; use a relist when the price is right but the listing has aged out of
view.

## Photography — the single biggest conversion lever

A listing's photos sell it before a word is read. The non-negotiables:

- **A clean, well-lit hero shot** on a neutral background — natural light,
  item filling the frame.
- **The tag / label / model number** — this is where the facts live (brand,
  size, material, model). Always capture it; it's also proof for the buyer.
- **Close-ups of any flaw**, shown honestly. A disclosed flaw photo *reduces*
  returns and builds the standing that lets you charge more next time.
- **Scale and detail** — hardware, construction, measurements laid flat.

When a seller shares only a hero shot, ask for the tag and a flaw close-up
before drafting. The photo set is the listing.

## Shipping basics

- **Poshmark** provides a flat-rate prepaid USPS Priority label; the buyer
  pays a fixed shipping fee, so the seller's shipping cost is effectively zero
  (just pack and drop).
- **eBay / Mercari** — the seller chooses calculated or flat shipping; weigh
  and measure honestly, because the platform's final-value fee is charged on
  shipping too. Build a small shipping kit (scale, poly mailers, boxes, tape).
- **Facebook Marketplace (local)** — no shipping at all; arrange a safe public
  pickup. This is the structural advantage of local: no label, no fee, no
  package.

## Taxes — the part sellers dread

Resale income is taxable, and marketplaces issue a **Form 1099-K** for
payments processed once the seller crosses the IRS reporting threshold (the
threshold has changed repeatedly in recent years — verify the current year's
figure, but plan as if any meaningful volume will be reported). Practical
hygiene the office supports:

- **Keep cost basis on every item** — you're taxed on profit (sale − cost −
  fees), not gross sales. Untracked cost basis means overpaying tax on resold
  personal items.
- **Track fees** — they're deductible against the sale.
- A clean ledger of cost, sale, fees, and dates is exactly what a year-end tax
  summary needs; the seller's resale board is that ledger.

(General guidance, not tax advice — a seller with real volume should confirm
the current 1099-K threshold and her situation with a professional.)

## Returns and disputes

- **Honest condition descriptions are the best return-prevention there is.** A
  stated flaw can't become a "not as described" claim.
- Know each platform's buyer-protection window and policy before promising
  "final sale" — on most platforms a buyer can open a case regardless.
- Respond to offers and messages quickly; speed of response correlates with
  conversion and with platform ranking.
