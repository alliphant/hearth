# Pricing and markdown strategy — open high, leave offer room, and nudge on a cadence

How to set the opening number, and — just as important — how to move it when
an item doesn't sell. Pricing isn't one decision; it's an opening move plus a
markdown plan.

## Set the opening price off USED, condition-matched SOLD comps

The anchor is always **what this item, secondhand and in roughly this
condition, has actually SOLD for** — not retail, not what other sellers are
*asking*. Filter comps to sold/completed listings, match the condition tier
(a "good, small flaw" piece prices off other good/fair sales, not pristine
ones), and exclude new-with-tags unless the item itself is NWT. Retail/MSRP is
useful for exactly one thing: the anchor in the copy ("retail $120, yours
$58"), never the basis for the ask.

## Pricing psychology, used on purpose

- **Charm pricing.** $49 reads cheaper than a round $50; $45 beats $46. End on
  9 or 5, avoid round tens. The office's markdown suggestions are already
  charm-priced for this reason.
- **Anchoring.** A crossed-out "retail $120" next to your $58 makes the price
  feel like a win.
- **Offer room.** On platforms with offers (Poshmark, eBay Best Offer, FB
  negotiation), list a touch high so the buyer's counter lands where you
  actually wanted to be. The "make an offer" buyer feels like they won; you
  hit your number.
- **OBO invites the message that starts the sale.** On Facebook, "firm" closes
  the door; "$45 OBO" opens a conversation that usually closes.
- **Bundle pricing** lifts average order value — on Poshmark especially, a
  bundle discount turns a $20 sale into a $50 one.

## The markdown cadence — the part most sellers skip

An item that hasn't sold isn't priced wrong forever; it's priced wrong *now*.
The discipline is to **move the price on a schedule instead of waiting and
hoping**:

- **Benchmark against how fast comparable items actually sell**, not a flat
  calendar. If the seller's jackets typically move in ~8 days, a jacket live
  for 19 is overdue; if her furniture takes ~40 days, day 19 is fine.
- **First nudge at ~1.5× the typical days-to-sell.** A modest **~10–12%**
  charm-priced drop is usually enough to re-surface the listing in sort/feed
  and re-trigger "price drop" notifications to people who liked or followed it.
- **Second, deeper cut at ~2.5×** the benchmark (call it "stale"): **~20%**
  off, or pair it with a relist/refresh.
- **A drop is also a marketing event.** On Poshmark a price drop to within a
  threshold sends offers to likers; on eBay it bumps recency. The markdown
  isn't just a lower number — it's free re-exposure. Time it deliberately.

The seller's office runs this as an **aging radar**: it flags active listings
past ~1.5× their category's typical days-to-sell and suggests a specific
charm-priced markdown. Raise the stalest one in conversation rather than
waiting to be asked — that proactive nudge is the difference between a price
list and a partner.

## Relisting vs. dropping

If an item is genuinely priced right but buried (old listing, low views),
sometimes the fix is **relist/refresh** rather than a price cut — a fresh
listing date and better lead photo can do what a markdown would, without
giving up margin. Reach for the markdown when comps say the price is high or
the item is simply overdue; reach for a relist when the price is fair but the
listing has gone stale in the feed.

## When the answer is "don't bother"

Sometimes the comps say the item isn't worth the effort to list, ship, and
manage. Say so plainly. The seller's return is on her *time*, not on listing
for its own sake — a $6 item that takes 30 minutes and nets $2 after fees is a
donation pile, not inventory.
